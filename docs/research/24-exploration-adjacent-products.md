# Map, journey, city, character: metaphor vs mechanic in language products

Date: 2026-08-22  
Scope: first-party product pages, help centres, blogs, App Store / Steam copy, and a few reputable reviews only where they describe a shipped mechanic. Not Reddit lore. Not an architecture proposal.

Product-direction synthesis (not a spec): [exploration-concept.md](../product-experience/exploration-concept.md). Streaks vs learning: [22](22-streaks-habits-gamification.md).

## Question

Which existing language or adjacent products already use map / journey / travel / city / character / discovery / expedition / story-world metaphors? What is the actual mechanic versus the marketing skin? For each: what they sell, what the learner does, whether geography is real, whether culture is authentic or stereotyped, whether it scales past A2, and what retention hooks they use (streaks and kin). Also: any first-party statement that *rejected* streaks.

## Bottom line

The travel/journey/city/character language is everywhere. The mechanic underneath is almost always one of four things: (1) a **linear lesson queue drawn as a path**; (2) **A1 travel-phrase topics** (hotel, café, airport) with no real map; (3) a **fictional character world** that keeps people tapping; or (4) **authentic media** (street video, imported podcasts, bilingual stories) with no geographic game at all. Real German cities as *content* exist (Easy German street interviews). Real cities as a *curriculum map you walk* do not. Nobody occupies “B1–B2 Study-band German in real places, with Noticing, where Progress is not a streak or a path pebble.”

## How to read this

Three different objects get sold as “exploration”:

| Object | What it is | Typical skin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Progress UI** | A sequenced course. The next lesson is the next circle / unit / voyage pick. | Path, journey, voyage, “My Journey” |
| **Situation catalogue** | Lessons titled Travel, Restaurant, Hotel. Geography is a topic list. | “Real-life conversations,” “for travel” |
| **World / character layer** | Mascots, storylines, VR rooms. Retention first; language second. | Characters, Adventures, AR teacher, RPG |
| **Authentic place as input** | Real streets, real speakers, real shows. No board you move a pawn on. | Street interviews, native videos, import |

A product can mix these. Duolingo mixes all four except authentic place. Easy German is almost only the last. Influent looks like exploration and is household-noun labelling.

## Duolingo: path, Stories, leagues, streaks, characters, Events, Adventures

**Metaphor sold.** A “clear path to follow on your learning journey”; a cast you fall in love with (explicit Zelda open-world analogy); Adventures as “the world of Duolingo”; Stories as quirky tales; weekly leagues; the beloved streak.

Sources: <https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/>; <https://blog.duolingo.com/building-character/>; <https://blog.duolingo.com/adventures/>; <https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/>.

**Mechanic.** The 2022 home-screen redesign replaced the skill tree with a **guided sequence of circles**. Each circle is one old “crown level.” Lesson *content* was the same, reordered for spaced mixing. Stories, practice sessions, and (later) Adventures sit on that sequence. The learner still does constrained items: tap tiles, translate, listen, speak to a prompt. Duolingo’s own FAQ for a busy day is “something quick to maintain your streak.” Unit titles were rewritten from opaque labels (“City 3”, “Travel 2”) to can-do phrases (“get directions”, “discuss destinations”) — travel as **topic labels on a path**, not a map of Germany.

Stories (shipped on web 2017, mobile later) are short illustrated dialogues with comprehension checks. First-party: “longer than the typical Duolingo lessons”; they covered A1–B1 at the 2020 write-up. They are **not** open reading: intermittent questions gate progress. Conversation mode in Stories was removed in the path redesign.

Characters were designed so learners would “spend time within our product.” They “speak” the sentence you translate; they play a cheer animation on a correct answer; mid-lesson interstitials reward a streak of correct items. Designers considered vikings and space aliens, then chose “modern humans but … quirky.” That is a **brand world**, not German public life. Adventures (2024) make this explicit: you play as Lily/Junior/Zari, move around an on-screen board, order coffee / grocery-shop / ask directions **inside the Duolingo universe**. Launch languages were English→French and Spanish→English, “more languages coming soon” — not a German city sim.

Leagues: weekly XP ranking, ten leagues, Diamond Tournament for top 10. Matching is by study habit and timezone, **not** by language. Opt-out exists (make profile private). First-party rationale: “Learning a new language takes a *really* long time, which is why we develop fun features (like Leaderboards!) to keep you engaged.”

Streaks: consecutive days with a completed lesson; freeze and gem repair. Help centre: the streak is “a proven way to motivate you to keep learning.” Historically, Duolingo restored streaks only for disaster-related cases; a June 2026 campaign let some 30+ day losers revive by doing three lessons (company LinkedIn / The Verge reporting of Duolingo’s own copy).

Events / Classes: the 2019 product blog still advertised in-person Events. Independent hosts and the Duolingo wiki report Classes/Events sunset on 18 January 2023. The cited help article (`Duolingo-Classes-Announcement-FAQ`) did not return body text when fetched for this note — treat the retirement as reported, not as a currently published first-party FAQ.

**Geography.** Not real. Path scenery and Adventures boards are product art. “City” in old unit names was a skill bucket.

**Culture.** Quirky-by-design. Formal/informal register in Adventures is taught as character relationships (Zari informal with Lily, formal with a receptionist), not as German *Sie/du* in a real city.

**Past A2.** First-party 2024: major courses including German now have content **through B2** (Duolingo Score 129). That is a content claim, not a spoken-interaction claim. Efficacy evidence in [02](02-app-outcomes-gap.md) still tops out around A2 recognition for typical completers of the older beginning courses.

**Hooks.** Streak, XP, hearts, leagues, Friends Quests, Legendary, characters, Stories/Radio as extra XP, Super/Max paywalls.

## Babbel: dialogue, real-life situations, cities?

**Metaphor sold.** “Shortest path to real-life conversations”; “Authenticity is key to fully exploring a new place via language”; language is “culture, history, people, and places”; Speak scenarios from coffee in Paris to greeting a German neighbour.

Sources: <https://www.babbel.com/the-babbel-method>; <https://www.babbel.com/press/en-us/releases/babbel-speak>; <https://www.babbel.com/learn-german>; <https://support.babbel.com/hc/en-us/articles/17020978582162-Streak>.

**Mechanic.** Expert-written 10-minute lessons: dialogue → practice → review (SRS-ish Review Manager). Native-speaker audio, not TTS. Grammar is in the lesson, not a cartoon path. Babbel Speak (2025 beta) is an **AI roleplay of a chosen scenario** with tasks and post-chat feedback — still a situation catalogue, not a city you walk. German marketing includes a “Learn German for Travel” slice (hotels, airports, restaurants) as **phrase focus**, not a map. No first-party “Babbel cities” product turned up.

**Geography.** Not real. Place names appear in marketing and scenario titles.

**Culture.** Native voices; first-party claims conversational lessons are “also lessons in culture.” That is still scripted dialogue, not street speech.

**Past A2.** Courses are sold Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced; German pages cite CEFR. Live small-group classes (when offered) have historically gone higher than the self-study app. First-party efficacy in [02](02-app-outcomes-gap.md) is novice Spanish, not B2 German.

**Hooks.** Daily streak (any activity counts: lesson, vocab, podcast, AI chat, grammar guide). Two streak freezes issued each week, unused ones do not stack.

## Busuu: community, travel situations

**Metaphor sold.** “Support you on your journey”; dedicated **Travel** side-courses (check into a hotel, metro tickets, restaurant); community of native speakers.

Sources: <https://www.busuu.com/en/course/learn-english-travel>; <https://www.busuu.com/en/course/learn-spanish-travel>; <https://help.busuu.com/hc/en-us/articles/16497935071633-What-s-a-streak>; <https://help.busuu.com/hc/en-gb/articles/16519527128849-How-many-languages-can-I-learn>.

**Mechanic.** CEFR-aligned bite-sized lessons (help copy: 3–5 minutes) covering four skills, then **Community Corrections**: you submit a written or spoken exercise and natives correct it. Travel is a **short specialised course**, then the Complete course continues. German Complete is listed **A1–C1**; German *certificates* are listed A1–B2 (help centre, language list vs certificate list — they are not the same claim).

**Geography.** Not a map. Travel = situation list. Community members have real locations; that is social matching, not a curriculum.

**Culture.** Native corrections can be authentic. Lesson videos of “real people” are claimed on the What-is-Busuu help page (fetch of that URL was Cloudflare-blocked; the travel course pages are live). Lesson situations are still textbook travel.

**Past A2.** Yes on the Complete-course claim (German to C1). Travel course itself is beginner survival.

**Hooks.** Blue-flame streak; weekly streak shields; Premium Plus streak repair once per 30 days. First-party: “once a streak is lost, it can’t be restored” (except via shields/repair).

## Memrise: natives, “Learn with Locals”, places, My Journey

**Metaphor sold.** “We bring the country to you”; traveler who “refuses to be that tourist”; videos of locals; **My Journey** as progress through “real-world stages.”

Sources: <https://www.memrise.com/>; <https://www.memrise.com/blog/major-update-a-new-version-of-the-app-is-coming>; <https://explore.memrise.com/new-experience>; <https://www.memrise.com/blog/team-update-full-walkthrough>; <https://explore.memrise.com/2023-learn-a-language-with-memrise>.

**Mechanic.** Core loop is **vocab + spaced repetition + short native-speaker clips**. “Learn with Locals” as a *standalone mode* was retired; clips now sit inside Learn/Review and a Videos tab (Memrise-made + YouTube). Conversations moved to MemBot (AI). Official community courses were split off. **My Journey** tracks recognised vs understood-in-context vs used-in-conversation — a **progress dashboard named as a journey**, not a street map. Wordlists are goal-based (travel, work, love). 2025 “new experience” first-party: they **removed points** (“Points might feel nice, but they don’t help you speak”) while **keeping streak and My Journey**.

**Geography.** Clips are filmed somewhere real; the product does not make you traverse those places. The retired iOS “Explore” feature is gone.

**Culture.** Stronger authenticity than cartoon apps: “slang, speed, emotion.” Still clip-length, not a city documentary, and not a B1 text sitting.

**Past A2.** Vocab/listening can go on; grammar depth is not the selling point. Exam-prep (IELTS, GCSE, CEFR-aligned tests) is a separate product line. First-party “basic conversation in 2 months at 15 minutes/day” is a speaking-confidence claim, not B2.

**Hooks.** Streak retained. Points rejected. MemBot, Videos tab XP-for-watching (older blog).

## Rosetta Stone: immersion path, travel scenarios, “no gimmicks”

**Metaphor sold.** “A path for real language learning”; Dynamic Immersion as child-like seeing/hearing; units include Greetings, **Travel**, Hobbies; “asking for help on a trip”; “no gimmicks needed.”

Sources: <https://www.rosettastone.com/our-method>; <https://eu.rosettastone.com/how-it-works/>; <https://blog.rosettastone.com/truaccent-learning-with-rosetta-stone/>.

**Mechanic.** Image–word matching, speak-into-TruAccent, write later. End-of-unit **Milestone**: two-way conversation with a **pre-recorded** native speaker. Optional live tutoring / Live Lessons on cultural topics. Audio Companion for commute. This is audiolingual-adjacent immersion, not a map.

**Geography.** Not real. Travel is a unit theme. Photos are stock-like teaching images, not Berlin streets.

**Culture.** First-party “immerses you in the language and its culture.” Independent workplace study (Nielson 2011, cited in [02](02-app-outcomes-gap.md)) found packages weak on culturally authentic tasks — that is independent, not first-party.

**Past A2.** Units continue; first-party efficacy headline is still “13 hours ≈ one college semester” (Vesselinov et al. 2019 on their method page). That is novice transfer, not B2.

**Hooks.** Progress through units; TruAccent as feedback. Explicit **anti-gimmick** marketing. No Duolingo-style league found in first-party method copy.

## Mondly / AR / VR: chatbot in a tourist set

**Metaphor sold.** “Travel the world from your couch”; train to Berlin, Spanish restaurant, hotel in Paris; virtual teacher in AR; virtual characters in VR.

Sources: Steam page <https://store.steampowered.com/app/1141930/Mondly_Learn_Languages_in_VR/>; PR <https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mondly-launches-the-first-augmented-reality-experience-that-uses-speech-recognition-to-teach-languages-677259213.html>. Mondly.com and several `/faq/` URLs returned 405 when fetched; Steam + PR are the first-party bodies used here. Search-indexed FAQ text (how conversations work; chatbot with speech recognition; VR “digitally rendered environment”) is consistent with Steam but is not a live fetch.

**Mechanic.** Main app: daily lessons + **scripted chatbot**. First-party FAQ (indexed): tap-and-hold mic to **repeat provided sentences**; speech recognition scores you. That is listen-and-repeat / pick-a-reply, not open conversation. VR/AR wrap the same idea in a 3D room or a hologram teacher. Steam: “dialogues inspired by authentic events” — named tourist cities, generic sets.

**Geography.** Named cities as **stage dressing**. Not OpenStreetMap. Not a real Bahnhof.

**Culture.** Tourist-script stereotype risk is high: restaurant, hotel, ticket. No first-party claim of filming real Berliners.

**Past A2.** Steam/marketing: ~40 practical topics / 30 languages / “fluency awaits.” That is a slogan. Reviews (not first-party) consistently call the chatbot branching and short. Do not treat those as company claims.

**Hooks.** Daily lessons, weekly quizzes, monthly challenges, calendar of daily lessons in a VR update (indexed FAQ). Streak-like daily return.

## Pimsleur: imagined travel conversation on audio

**Metaphor sold.** “Journey to newfound linguistic knowledge”; “conversing … like you were born in Berlin”; Learn German for Travel (hotels, tours, restaurants); “order schnitzel in Stuttgart or dash around Dresden.”

Sources: <https://www.pimsleur.com/the-pimsleur-method/>; <https://www.pimsleur.com/learn-german/>; <https://www.pimsleur.com/learn-german/lifetime-subscription/>.

**Mechanic.** 30-minute audio: hear a dialogue, then **anticipate** the next line (pause, speak, hear confirmation). Graduated Interval Recall, Core Vocabulary, Organic Learning (every item in a conversational exchange). Hands-free / driving mode. Premium extras: flashcards, Speak Easy roleplay of the **same** dialogues, Voice Coach AI, ~100 reading lessons, **“30 spectacular high resolution travel photographs”** and “30 light bulb moments” of culture/history per German Premium level.

**Geography.** Imagined. Photos decorate. You do not navigate Dresden.

**Culture.** Audio cultural asides + photo pack. First-party still sells schnitzel/Stuttgart/Dresden as travel colour. The spoken situations are generic (directions, restaurants), which is honest survival language, not a city documentary.

**Past A2.** German: **5 levels × 30 core lessons** (~75 hours of core audio) plus reading. First-party: “first words to confident everyday conversation.” That is a conversational-survival ceiling, not CEFR B2 reading of native media.

**Hooks.** One lesson a day is the method (FAQ: do not take notes during the audio). App progress tracking, certificates via Voice Coach. Not a flame-streak product in the method essay; daily cadence is pedagogical, then productized.

## Mango Languages: conversation construction, culture notes

**Metaphor sold.** “Real Voices. Real Progress”; “designed for the real world”; libraries “exploration of the world”; Intuitive Language Construction™ including **culture** as a sixth skill.

Sources: <https://mangolanguages.com/>; <https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/344141/Mango%20Languages%E2%80%99%20approach%20to%20Second%20Language%20Acquisition%20-%20Mango%20Classroom%20Edition%20(General).pdf>; support <https://support.mangolanguages.com/hc/en-us/articles/21976120160923-Lessons-K12-Teachers>.

**Mechanic.** Chapter opens with a **full conversation**, then deconstructs into vocab/grammar/culture notes and rebuilds. Memory-building and translation-style exercises. Classroom/Fresco (Spanish) is the same idea with teacher decks. This is communicative-courseware, not a map.

**Geography.** Not real.

**Culture.** First-party: short culture notes on “little c” customs (described in FLTMAG’s walkthrough of the product; Mango’s own SLA PDF says culture is the fifth skill). Notes are textbook sidebars, not street footage.

**Past A2.** Multi-chapter courses; K12 Fresco is multi-year Spanish. Consumer German depth is not specified on the homepage beyond “70+ languages.”

**Hooks.** Progress through chapters. Library/education distribution (show-up via institution), not leagues.

## LingoDeer: grammar path + “Story” as unit recap

**Metaphor sold.** “Engaging stories” with “interesting cultural facts”; fluency and mastery.

Sources: <https://www.lingodeer.com/>; help <https://support.lingodeer.com/en/support/solutions/articles/61000278827-what-is-the-story-section->; <https://support.lingodeer.com/en/support/solutions/articles/61000194372-how-is-lingodeer-different-from-other-apps->.

**Mechanic.** Teacher-written **grammar-first** modules with Learning Tips, then games/quizzes. The Story (or Conversation Practice) section is **extension material after the unit**: reading/listening questions plus read-aloud. Fluent module (some languages): more dialogue practice. Not an open world.

**Geography.** Not real.

**Culture.** “Cultural facts” inside stories — first-party does not claim they are filmed in-country.

**Past A2.** Built as beginner→intermediate classroom-in-an-app. Fluent is extra conversation, not a native-media gym.

**Hooks.** Lesson completion, review tab, flashcards, gamified quizzes. Streak not emphasised on the marketing homepage (absence in fetched copy, not a rejection).

## Drops: five minutes, visual vocab, Language Voyage

**Metaphor sold.** Language Voyage = “personal language learning GPS”; “set sail”; “you’re the captain”; topic categories include Travel Talk.

Sources: <https://languagedrops.com/>; <https://languagedrops.com/blog/language-voyage-guided-learning-path>; help <https://support.languagedrops.com/hc/en-us/articles/23836093365651-Language-Voyage>; <https://support.languagedrops.com/hc/en-us/articles/19405460893715-Streaks-in-Drops>; Kahoot family page <https://kahoot.com/home/learning-apps/drops/>.

**Mechanic.** Swipe/match **image–word** games, 5 minutes (free). Spaced repetition in Review Dojo after 50 words. Grammar recently added for some languages including German (homepage banner). **Language Voyage** is a **guided sequence of vocab units**: finish one, pick among three next units by interest. Checkpoint = 20 questions, three lives. That is a Duolingo-path analogue for word lists, not a map of Köln.

**Geography.** Not real. “Forest without a map” in their blog is a metaphor for too many topics.

**Culture.** Illustrated icons. First-party does not claim authentic video.

**Past A2.** Vocab volume (5,000+ words/phrases claimed) can serve advanced *lexis*; there is no B2 text sitting. Grammar is a late add-on.

**Hooks.** Streak + Streak Rescue (free: 1; Premium: up to 4). Kahoot marketing also says missing a day won’t lose your “score streak” — help centre is stricter (rescue then reset). Monthly Challenges.

## HelloTalk and Tandem: social geography, not a curriculum map

**Metaphor sold.** HelloTalk: “Talk to the World”; “Search the World”; “Your Language Learning Journey Starts Here”; match by **city**. Tandem: “Any Time. Any Place”; take the community “on your travels.”

Sources: <https://www.hellotalk.com/en>; <https://www.hellotalk.com/en/features/language-exchange>; <https://www.tandem.net/>; <https://tandem.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043364712-What-is-Tandem-and-how-does-it-work>; <https://tandem.net/blog/tandem-app-overview>.

**Mechanic.** Language exchange: text, voice, video; corrections; translation tools. HelloTalk: Moments feed, Voicerooms, timed Language Exchange splits. Tandem: application review for community safety, Language Parties (audio rooms), Pro filters. **City is a search facet**, not a lesson. Preparing-for-a-trip copy means “we’ll match you with locals from your destination.”

**Geography.** Real people, real hometowns. No pawn on a map. Quality varies with who you find.

**Culture.** As authentic as the partner. Also: dating-app failure mode (not first-party; do not treat as a feature).

**Past A2.** Scales if you can already produce. Weak as an A0 course. No CEFR path.

**Hooks.** Notifications, community, Pro limits. No first-party streak article found for Tandem language exchange (do not confuse with unrelated “Tandem Daily Games” puzzle apps). HelloTalk marketing uses “journey,” not a flame help article in the fetches for this note.

## StoryLearning / Olly Richards: story-world as the syllabus

**Metaphor sold.** Learn through stories, not rules; “your brain is wired for story”; Narnia analogy for new scripts; “journey of a thousand miles” as a cliché he then mocks.

Sources: <https://storylearning.com/>; <https://storylearning.com/learn-language-stories>; <https://storylearning.com/blog/10-rules-storylearning>; <https://courses.storylearning.com/p/home>.

**Mechanic.** A **graded narrative** carries the syllabus. Chapter: read+listen → video “guided discovery” of forms in that chapter → exercises on the same story. Rule #4: reread so you notice more. Rule #3: don’t study grammar first. Rule #6: don’t look up every word. Speaking is a later “Activate” step (tutor talk *about the story*), not tiles. This is the closest first-party cousin to Davio’s “interesting text + noticing,” except the text is a **purpose-written story**, not Gym-sitting German sourced from owner-approved real-ish Study-band pieces.

**Geography.** Fictional (or tourist-plot) story world. Not a city product.

**Culture.** Depends on the story. Not street-authentic by design; it is level-controlled.

**Past A2.** Courses exist at multiple levels; Short Stories books similarly. Method *can* scale; each course is still one story + explanations.

**Hooks.** “Learn every day” (rule #2) as a habit sermon, **not** a streak freeze shop. Marketing: “Forget about … silly apps.” No first-party streak feature described on the method pages.

## LingQ: “live it” / import anything / measured journey

**Metaphor sold.** “Don’t just study a language, live it”; “learn wherever life takes you”; “enjoy the journey” (new-learner guide). Progress = words known, not a map.

Sources: <https://www.lingq.com/en/>; <https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-languages-like-steve-kaufmann/>; <https://forum.lingq.com/t/lingq-overview-how-it-works/8696>.

**Mechanic.** Read+listen, click unknown words into LingQs, SRS-ish review, **import** podcasts/YouTube/Netflix/ebooks. Library + user content. First-party contrast: not “repeating a small number of strange phrases or playing games.” Stats and **language challenges** keep you “on track.” Forum support (staff) documents a **streak tied to daily LingQ-creation goals** — they did add a streak, and some users objected. Kaufmann (podcast, not a paper): Duolingo’s success is engagement/streak; a year of Spanish streak can still mean a small word count because items are unconnected.

**Geography.** Only if you import content about a place. No map UI as curriculum.

**Culture.** As authentic as the imported media. Beginner library is often learner-made / simplified.

**Past A2.** First-party: works at all levels; import is the advanced move. A user testimonial cites DELF B1 after a year — anecdote on their homepage, not a trial.

**Hooks.** Statistics, challenges, streak-on-daily-LingQs, word-count milestones. Philosophy anti-game; product still counts days.

## Fluent Forever: pronunciation → image cards → tutors

**Metaphor sold.** Little journey language. Four steps: ears, image vocab, “grammar naturally through stories relevant to you,” tutors. App has a **Daily Streak Task** (flame on Home).

Sources: <https://fluent-forever.com/>; help <https://help.fluent-forever.com/hc/en-us/articles/40234050300443-Why-did-my-streak-break>; book/method site <https://www.fluent-forever.com/index.php>.

**Mechanic.** Minimal-pair ear training, monolingual image SRS, then live coaching. Stories in the four-step marketing are **personalised grammar-in-context**, not a published story-world product like StoryLearning. Help centre: streak continues only if the **Daily Streak Task** is done by local midnight — studying other things in the app may not count.

**Geography.** Not a map product.

**Culture.** Pronunciation of the target language; tutor-dependent after that.

**Past A2.** Book/app as foundation; tutors for speech. Not a B2 content library.

**Hooks.** Streak task, SRS due cards, coaching schedule. They **have** streaks; they did not reject them.

## Easy German: real streets, real cities (not an app loop)

**Metaphor sold.** “Learning German from the Streets.”

Source: <https://www.easygerman.org/> (homepage copy also confirmed via search snippets when a timed fetch failed once). Easy Languages project blurb on episode pages: authentic street interviews; original + English subtitles.

**Mechanic.** YouTube/podcast: interview people in German cities at real speed (Easy German) or slower (Super Easy German); dual subtitles. Membership: Discord, transcripts, exercises, book clubs, conversation members. **This is authentic-place input.** It is not a gym sitting with a Gap map. The learner watches; structure is editorial (weekly shows, membership challenges), not a path of pebbles.

**Geography.** **Real.** Different German cities, real locations.

**Culture.** As authentic as whoever stops on the street, plus host framing. Stereotypes possible in topic choice; speech itself is not a cartoon waiter.

**Past A2.** First-party “Where to begin?” includes **Upper Intermediate (B2)**. Native-speed interviews are often *harder* than Study-band; Super Easy German is the slower companion. Matches Davio’s Immersion-vs-Study split more than any app path.

**Hooks.** YouTube cadence, membership community, 2026 “challenges” mentioned on-channel. No Duolingo flame in first-party homepage copy.

## Travel phrase apps (TripLingo and kin)

**TripLingo.** Company LinkedIn: travel language tool, phrasebook/culture/Wi-Fi dialer era, 12 languages, multi-device. Independent software listings still describe voice translation + etiquette. **No live first-party product page with a current mechanic was fetched.** Treat as a **phrasebook + culture tips** category, not a city-learning game. Do not invent 2026 features.

**Pimsleur / Babbel / Busuu / Memrise “for travel”** (above) are the surviving first-party version of this category: **survival dialogues**, not exploration.

## Games people confuse with learning

### Influent (Three Flip / playinfluent.com)

**Metaphor sold.** Explore a 3D apartment; learn by clicking the world; “freedom”; no linear path.

Sources: <https://playinfluent.com/>; Steam <https://store.steampowered.com/app/274980/Influent_Language_Learning_Game/>; press kit <https://threeflip.com/presskit-influent/>.

**Mechanic.** One **generic modern apartment**. Click objects → noun/adjective/verb + native audio. ~400–500 items per language pack, including German. Research origin: Tsukuba “3D dictionary” (SanjigenJiten). **Exploration is real as a 3D UI; learning is labelling furniture.** No grammar of German case beyond whatever the citation form is. No city.

**Past A2.** No. Household vocab.

**Hooks.** Collect/master words, optional tasks. Explicitly no time pressure.

### Learn Japanese To Survive! Hiragana Battle (Steam)

**Metaphor sold.** Full RPG: quests, battles, story. Learning Japanese is the plot.

Source: Steam <https://store.steampowered.com/app/438270/Learn_Japanese_To_Survive_Hiragana_Battle/>.

**Mechanic.** Chapter teaches a hiragana set; **combat is a quiz**. Grammar/phrases as a bonus. Trilogy continues into katakana/kanji. This is **script drill inside an RPG skin**. German has no analogue in that series. Do not confuse “I explored a fantasy map” with language acquisition past kana.

**Past A2.** No (by design: one kana set).

### Mondly VR / IMMERSE

Mondly VR: tourist-set chatbot (above). **IMMERSE** (<https://immerse.com/>): enterprise “Fluency Performance” in VR/mobile/AI glasses — **simulated work/social environments with instructors**, not a hobby German gym. Adjacent as “virtual place,” different customer.

## Streaks: who uses them, who actually rejected them

**Shipped streaks (first-party help or product copy):** Duolingo, Babbel, Busuu, Drops, Fluent Forever, LingQ (daily LingQ goal), Memrise (kept through the 2025 redesign). Several add freezes/shields/rescue so the number rarely dies.

**No first-party “we rejected streaks” from a major language app was found.** Closest statements:

| Who | What they actually said |
| --- | --- |
| **Memrise** (2025 new experience) | Rejected **points**, not streaks: “Points might feel nice, but they don’t help you speak.” Streak is explicitly preserved. |
| **Rosetta Stone** method page | “You’ll feel inspired to keep learning … **no gimmicks needed.**” Anti-Duolingo positioning, not a streak changelog. |
| **LingQ** homepage / Kaufmann method | Against “gamified drilling apps” and “playing games.” The product still has challenges, stats, and a streak on daily LingQs (forum staff). Philosophy ≠ streak deletion. |
| **StoryLearning** | “Forget about … silly apps.” Habit = read every day. No freeze shop. |
| **Language Transfer** | No accounts, ads, or data sales. Streaks are absent because there is no engagement product, not because of a published anti-streak essay. |
| **KnowMine** (not a language app) | Explicit “Why … Rejected Streak Counters.” Out of category; listed only so this note does not pretend a language vendor wrote that post. <https://knowmine.ai/en/blog/ai-knowledge-base-reward-without-streak> |

**Duolingo did not reject streaks.** It doubled down (freeze, repair, 2026 revival event). First-party efficacy framework still puts daily return first ([02](02-app-outcomes-gap.md)).

## What is still unoccupied as a product space

The crowded pattern is **travel as A1 situations**, **journey as a progress bar**, and **characters/VR as a reason to open the app**. The rare pattern is **real German places as the message** (Easy German), and even that is a YouTube/podcast feed: watch, maybe join Discord, no Noticing sitting, no Grammar Gap map, no owner-approved Study-band constraint. StoryLearning occupies “one compelling narrative + guided discovery” but the world is invented and the unit of progress is the course chapter. LingQ/FluentU occupy “any real media you import” without a geographic or character metaphor and without a curated B1–B2 German gym. Nobody ships: *this* Berlin/Köln/everyday-Germany text, at Study-band difficulty, as the sitting, with form noticing, where the map (if any) is the learner’s grammar gaps — not a path of café pebbles, not Duo’s friends, not a tourist VR hotel. That combination is still empty. Occupying it with another streak, another path, or another cartoon city would be entering the occupied half.

## Implications for Davio (discovery only)

1. **Do not ship a path-of-circles or a cartoon city.** That slot is filled. Duolingo even renamed “City 3” because the metaphor was empty. A Gap map is a different object: it records forms the Learner does not control, not the next café unit.

2. **Do not ship Duo-class characters as the reason to open the Gym.** First-party Duolingo is explicit: characters exist to increase time-in-product. Adventures practice register inside *their* universe. Easy German already owns real German faces on real streets.

3. **Travel-as-A1-catalogue is also filled** (Babbel, Busuu Travel, Pimsleur, Mondly hotel, Memrise traveler persona). A B1–B2 learner who lives in Germany does not need another “order coffee” tree. If a piece is set in a city, the job is the *text*, not the destination sticker.

4. **Authentic place as input is not empty, but it is not a product sitting.** Easy German (and FluentU/LingQ imports) already supply streets and shows. The hole is wrapping Study-band, owner-approved pieces with Noticing — which [03](03-mvp-activity-feasibility.md) already recommended — without stealing Easy German’s feed or Duolingo’s flame.

5. **If Davio ever uses “journey” language, keep it honest:** the journey is through *this piece* (read → notice → mark), not across a map of Germany and not across consecutive calendar days. StoryLearning’s reread rule is the useful mechanic; their fictional novel is optional, not required.

6. **Streak rejection is still available as a product stance** because no major language app published “we deleted streaks on purpose.” Memrise deleted *points* and kept the flame. Occupying “Progress ≠ XP” ([02](02-app-outcomes-gap.md)) is still a differentiator only if the UI does not reintroduce a day-counter as the main signal.
