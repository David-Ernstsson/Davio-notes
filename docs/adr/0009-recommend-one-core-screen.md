# Recommend a core learner screen

Owner-locked 2026-09-21; revised 2026-09-26 for [issue #94](https://github.com/David-Ernstsson/Davio/issues/94). The founder approves the few core screens. The team owns the brief, exploration, internal review, and recommendation before a screen reaches the founder. Each proposal continues the governing screen record and names the change it tests. The existing learner journey and approved screen behavior remain authority.

## Brief and exploration

Start with the Learner's one job in this part of the inhabited Target-language world, the governing journey and screen record, the approved behavior, the shared visual kit, and one question the still should answer. Record visible controls and implied transitions as approved, provisional, or unresolved in the mockup's existing `QUESTION.md` or current spec. Name any conflict as a proposed change before drawing it. Use the current journey index and [screen consistency](../product-experience/screen-consistency.md); a rejected mockup is history, not a fresh brief.

Explore privately as needed. For one representative screen whose visual direction remains open, trial **two polished, coherent alternatives**. They answer the same brief and differ on one meaningful visual question. Each is a complete screen using the working kit, not a fragment for the founder to combine with another. The team states which it recommends and why. This trial revises the earlier one-recommendation delivery rule; it does not reopen settled screen behavior or the owner-selected dark-moss working direction. Preserve a selected direction while refining details or making it interactive unless the founder reopens it. After the founder decides on that screen, the team briefly assesses whether the second polished candidate improved the decision enough to justify its cost. The founder then decides whether to keep, narrow, or end the pair handoff for later screens.

## Internal gate

A UX/design reviewer who did not make the candidate checks it before founder review. Return a candidate that contradicts the governing screen record, loses Davio's learner job or inhabited-world identity, has a generic or incoherent visual direction, breaks the shared kit without a reason, or has an unusable shorter-phone composition. These are hard failures. The maker gets **one** repair attempt per failed candidate; if it still fails, report the concrete reason and stop revising that candidate for this handoff. Close calls about visual expression are design judgments: explain them with the recommendation rather than turning taste into a checklist.

If both pass, present both. If only one passes, present the passing candidate as the recommendation and explain the other's failure. That one-candidate result cannot show whether the pair helped. Never show a failed candidate as a choice or silently start another repair loop.

## Founder handoff and evidence

Show the actual stills at the intended phone size and a shorter phone, with a short decision brief: the Learner job, the open visual question, the team's preferred candidate and tradeoff, relevant unresolved behavior, and what remains unproven. Keep the rule map and internal review notes with the artifact, available on request. A still can establish visible hierarchy, composition, crops, wrapping, and apparent clearance at the rendered sizes. It cannot establish tap discoverability, transition or resume behavior, actual hit areas, Android system insets, accessibility, or learning outcomes. Visual acceptance is separate from those later checks.

At the founder's request, a reviewer who neither made nor internally cleared the candidate gives an independent critique **before** the founder states a preference. Review the candidate or pair the founder names. A comparison assesses both against the same brief. Return located findings, likely Learner consequences, evidence or a stated heuristic, and a recommendation. The founder keeps the visual decision.

## Why this handoff

The earlier commercial-fidelity demo let the founder taste-gate three moments and let the team own the screens between them. That did not address weak, crowded, off-spec, or one-off screens reaching the founder. Showing two polished directions may make an open choice clearer, but costs more and risks asking the founder to assemble a design. A separate internal gate, complete alternatives, one team recommendation, and a one-screen trial keep the quality responsibility with the team while letting the founder judge a meaningful choice.
